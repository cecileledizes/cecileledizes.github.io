About

I study how the ocean's tides, flowing over underwater mountains, generate internal waves that carry energy across ocean basins and ultimately fuel deep-ocean mixing.

I completed my PhD in 2025 at the Institut de Mécanique des Fluides de Toulouse (IMFT), supervised by Matthieu Mercier and Olivier Thual, in collaboration with Nicolas Grisouard (University of Toronto). During my PhD I developed a three-dimensional boundary element method for internal tide generation by isolated seamounts. I also worked on the formation of density staircases in rotating, stratified turbulence during the Woods Hole Geophysical Fluid Dynamics program.

Before my PhD, I graduated as an engineer from ISAE-Supaéro (Toulouse).

Research

Internal tides

Internal tide generation by 3-D seamounts

When the barotropic tide flows over seamounts, part of its energy is converted into internal waves. I developed a boundary element method that solves this problem in three dimensions for arbitrary isolated topographies, with background rotation and finite depth, and without restrictions on the size or steepness of the seamount. It gives local maps of conversion and the direction of the radiated energy flux, which matter for parameterising tidal mixing in climate models.

Stratified turbulence

Density layering in rotating, stratified turbulence

Stirred stratified fluids can spontaneously organise into staircases: well-mixed layers separated by sharp density interfaces. I investigated how such layering forms when rotation and stratification act together, a question I started during the Geophysical Fluid Dynamics program at Woods Hole.

Software

Python · MPI · PETSc

ITIDES-3D

Parallel boundary element solver for internal tide generation by three-dimensional isolated seamounts. It computes the source distribution on the topography, then reconstructs modal velocities, pressure, energy fluxes and conversion rates. Open source under GPL-3.0.
